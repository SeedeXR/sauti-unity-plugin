The Frostmere is a wind-scoured peninsula in the far north, settled three centuries ago by exiles who refused to swear fealty to the southern crowns. Its people are sparse, stubborn, and slow to trust strangers. The land freezes for eight months of the year.

Two powers shape daily life here. The first is the weather itself: blizzards arrive without warning and travellers who underestimate the cold do not return. The second is the slow leak of old magic from the ruins beneath the ice. Where the magic surfaces, strange things grow.

Most settlements cluster along the coast where the sea keeps the worst of the cold at bay. The largest of these is Stormwall, named for the gale that batters its harbour every autumn. Inland, the few villages that survive do so by hunting, trapping, and trading furs.

The Frostmere has no king. Disputes are settled by elder councils and, when those fail, by combat. This is not romanticised — it is grim, brief, and rarely just. But it is the only law the peninsula has ever known.
