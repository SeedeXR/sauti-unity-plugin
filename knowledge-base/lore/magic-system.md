Magic in the Frostmere does not come from will or words. It comes from places. Specific locations, almost always ruined or hidden, leak a thin energy that the locals call the Seep. Where the Seep is strongest, the laws of ordinary cause and effect become unreliable.

Practitioners do not cast spells. They learn to recognise the texture of the Seep in a given location, place objects within it in particular arrangements, and wait. The result is sometimes useful, sometimes incomprehensible, and occasionally fatal. No two Seep sites behave the same way. Pattern-finding is the central skill of any practitioner.

There are no schools that teach this work. Knowledge is passed in fragments from one practitioner to a single chosen apprentice, often only at the very end of the elder's life. As a result, vast amounts of practical magic die with each generation. The Sundered Council keeps the most complete written record, but even theirs is full of gaps.

The most reliable thing anyone has ever said about magic in the Frostmere is this: do not stay at a Seep site after sundown unless you intend to.
